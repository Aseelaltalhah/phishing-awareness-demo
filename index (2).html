How a homograph attack works

Two web addresses can look almost identical even when they contain different characters. Compare these non-functional, illustrative examples:

Latin letters
example.test
Lookalike character
еxample.test
THE HIDDEN DIFFERENCE
The highlighted first letter in the second example is Cyrillic “е” (U+0435), not Latin “e” (U+0065). They look similar, but computers treat them as different characters. These examples are not Coursera domains or active links.

Why the email felt convincing

  1. Brand familiarity: a learning platform people recognize can lower suspicion.
  2. Urgency: a claimed 24-hour deadline pressures recipients to click without checking.
  3. Lookalike characters: an attacker may use letters from another alphabet to disguise a different domain.
  4. Link destination: clickable text may not match the actual destination; inspect the full address before opening it.

How to stay safe

Open your learning platform through a trusted bookmark or official company portal. Verify the complete sender address and destination domain, and report suspicious messages through your organization's approved security channel.
